A Mac remote on your iPhone, for the Mac mini under your TV

A Mac mini on the TV makes a great media box, right up until you need a mouse. Here's what works from the sofa, and what to know before you set it up.

Pointer and click, on a big screen

Move the pointer across the TV with your thumb and tap to click. Lost it somewhere on a 65-inch screen? Center Pointer brings it back to the middle (Full App).

Search with a real keyboard

Type titles and web addresses on your phone's own keyboard. Live sends each key as you type; Draft lets you fix typos before anything reaches the Mac.

Streaming sites in a browser

Services you watch in Safari or Chrome are web pages, so a pointer and keyboard handle them fine: click play, pick an episode, and Space pauses most web video players.

Apps, windows and Mac volume

Open apps from the Launcher and jump between windows without a keyboard. Volume up, volume down and mute are available as shortcut buttons. Both are part of Full App.

Before you set it up

  • Range. Bluetooth gives the smoothest pointer and is the recommended connection, but it's a short-range radio. Walls, a closed TV cabinet or a Mac tucked behind the screen can weaken it. If the pointer stutters or drops, move the Mac into the open, or switch to Wi-Fi in the app's Trackpad & Pointer settings with both devices on the same home network.
  • Make the pointer easy to see. On the Mac, System Settings, Accessibility, Display lets you make the pointer bigger. From across a room, it helps a lot.
  • Keep the phone dim. In the app's Settings, under Look, pick Dark. It uses warm, low-glare colors, so your screen doesn't light up the room mid-movie.
  • Sound over HDMI. If the Mac sends audio to the TV over HDMI, macOS may not be able to change the volume at all. In that case the TV's own remote stays in charge of volume.
  • It controls the Mac, not the TV. Power, inputs and the TV's built-in apps still need the TV remote.

Full setup steps, including Bluetooth vs Wi-Fi, are in the iPhone as a mouse guide. Comparing options? See how we stack up against Remote Mouse, Mobile Mouse and Unified Remote.

A remote for the whole Mac

Media-app remotes give up the moment a dialog pops up. A real pointer and keyboard never do.

Thumb-only operation

Drive the pointer, tap to click, flick to scroll — all with the thumb of the hand that's holding the phone. The other hand is for snacks.

Anything on screen, not one app

YouTube, Plex, a browser, that "Update later?" dialog — it's a pointer and a keyboard, so the whole screen is fair game. Switch from the movie to music without standing up.

Search without the squint-walk

Type the movie title on your phone instead of pecking at an on-screen keyboard with arrow keys. Draft it in the composer, send it over, done.

No ads between you and pause

Some remote apps show you a banner ad before they let you click. This one is a remote, not a billboard — no ads, no account, ever.
