Who this is for

Anyone for whom a keyboard and mouse ask more of their hands than they want to give right now. You don't need a diagnosis or a reason.

What macOS already gives you, and where it falls short

macOS has solid built-in accessibility tools for one-handed use. They're free and worth knowing. Here's what each one does, and the gap it leaves when you're not sitting right at the keyboard.

Sticky Keys

Press modifier keys one at a time instead of all at once, so Shift-Command-4 becomes Shift, then Command, then 4. It's in System Settings, Accessibility, Keyboard. Apple's guide

Where it falls short: you still need to be at the keyboard and reach keys spread across it, and it does nothing for the pointer.

Accessibility Keyboard

An on-screen keyboard for using the Mac without a physical one, with custom panels you can build in the Panel Editor. Apple's guide

Where it falls short: it takes up part of the Mac's screen, and you need a way to point and click to use it.

Dwell

Rest the pointer on something and a click, double-click, right-click or drag happens after a short countdown. It's designed for head- and eye-tracking. Apple's guide

Where it falls short: every action waits for a timer, and you still need something to move the pointer.

Pointer Control

Mouse Keys moves the pointer from the keyboard or number pad. Alternate pointer actions click using keys, switches or facial expressions. Head pointer moves the pointer with your head, using the Mac's camera. Apple's guide

Where it falls short: Mouse Keys is slow over long distances and needs the keyboard, and head pointer needs you facing the camera, close to the Mac.

Tip: Option-Command-F5 opens the Accessibility Shortcuts panel so you can switch these on and off quickly. On a Mac or Magic Keyboard with Touch ID, pressing Touch ID three times does the same, which is easier with one hand.

How Control My Mac fills the gaps

It puts the Mac's pointer and keyboard on the phone in your hand, so you can sit wherever is comfortable and still do everything.

Pointing with one finger

Drag a thumb to move the pointer, tap to click, double- or triple-tap, press and hold to drag. Mouse Hold keeps the button down for you, so a long drag doesn't need constant pressure.

Chords become single taps

Tap Command once to latch it on, then tap the next key. Tap Command again to release it. Ready-made buttons cover copy, paste, undo, Spotlight, screenshots and switching apps.

Your own shortcuts, learned from the Mac

Put any shortcut on a Grid button, recorded from the Mac's keyboard or entered by hand. Assign a Grid to an app and it appears whenever that app is in front. More in the Grid guide.

Type, then send

Write in the Draft keyboard at your own pace, with the phone's autocorrect and dictation, then insert the text on the Mac in one go. Open apps from the Launcher instead of hunting in the Dock.
